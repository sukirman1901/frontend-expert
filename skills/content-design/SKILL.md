# Content Design

## Overview

Interface copy helps a person understand, act, and recover. Preserve product truth; clarity outranks generic enthusiasm.

## When to use

- Labels, buttons, help, empty/error/success states, or confirmations
- Onboarding, product terminology, voice, or localization readiness
- README, changelog, docs, or marketing copy that reads like generic AI output

## When to skip

- Visual text rendering → `design-typography`
- Validation mechanics → `forms-validation`
- Landing section structure → `marketing-landing`

## MUST

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Preserve facts** | Never invent capabilities, metrics, testimonials, or guarantees |
| **Action-led** | Buttons name the action; errors provide a next step when one exists |
| **Consequence clarity** | Destructive confirmation names object, outcome, and reversibility |
| **Consistent terms** | One concept keeps one name across the flow |
| **Localization-ready** | Avoid concatenation and layout-dependent wording |
| **Human specificity** | Remove filler, hype, fake quotes, and vague AI abstractions |

## Workflow

1. **Inventory** — audience, task, product facts, terminology, tone, and affected states.
2. **Diagnose** — ambiguity, missing recovery, inconsistency, hype, or translation risk.
3. **Rewrite** — lead with user meaning, action, consequence, or recovery.
4. **Fit** — test realistic lengths without deleting necessary meaning.
5. **Verify** — show before/after, preserve facts, and test the complete flow.

## Boundaries

- **May decide:** concise wording consistent with established product voice.
- **Must not:** fabricate proof, shorten away consequences, or use visual truncation to solve weak copy.

## Checklist

- [ ] Audience, facts, terminology, and states identified
- [ ] Actions, errors, empty states, and destructive consequences are clear
- [ ] Copy survives expansion and preserves meaning
- [ ] Before/after and rationale reported for review work
